# Datasheet for the BBO Capstone Dataset

*Following the Datasheets for Datasets framework (Gebru et al., 2021)*

---

## Motivation

**For what purpose was the dataset created?**  
To support a structured Bayesian black-box optimisation (BBO) capstone project within the Imperial College London Executive Master in Machine Learning & Artificial Intelligence. The dataset provides initial observations for eight synthetic unknown functions, serving as the starting point for iterative GP-based optimisation across sequential submission rounds.

**Who created the dataset and on whose behalf?**  
Created and curated by Imperial College London as part of the BBO Capstone project portal. Accessed and used by Gian Franco Cattaneo (COO, SALOV S.p.A.) as course participant.

**Any other comments?**  
The dataset is intentionally opaque — the analytical form of each function is unknown to the participant. This mirrors real-world black-box scenarios (proprietary algorithms, complex simulations, biological processes).

---

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?**  
Each instance is an input–output pair: a d-dimensional vector `x ∈ [0, 0.999999]^d` (the query point) and a scalar `y = f(x)` (the function output). All eight functions are treated as maximisation problems.

**How many instances are there in total?**

| Function | d | Initial observations (N₀) | Submitted queries (R1–R10) | Total (est.) |
|----------|---|--------------------------|---------------------------|-------------|
| f1 | 2 | 10 | 10 | 20 |
| f2 | 2 | 10 | 10 | 20 |
| f3 | 3 | 15 | 10 | 25 |
| f4 | 4 | 30 | 10 | 40 |
| f5 | 4 | 20 | 10 | 30 |
| f6 | 5 | 20 | 10 | 30 |
| f7 | 6 | 30 | 10 | 40 |
| f8 | 8 | 40 | 10 | 50 |

*Initial N₀ values are estimates from session history; exact counts confirmed by portal .npy files.*

**Does the dataset contain all possible instances or is it a sample?**  
It is a sparse sample of a continuous input space. The true function domain is [0, 0.999999]^d for each function. Even at N=50 (f8), the space is vastly undersampled at 8 dimensions — roughly 50 points in a unit 8-cube represents negligible coverage.

**Does the dataset contain data that might be considered confidential?**  
No. All data is synthetic, generated by the portal for academic purposes.

**Is any information missing from individual instances?**  
No missing values. All submitted queries return a single scalar output from the portal without exceptions or timeouts.

**Are there any known errors, sources of noise, or redundancies?**  
- **Observation noise**: present at low levels in some functions (WhiteKernel noise level fits 1e-4 to 0.1 across functions).  
- **Near-duplicate queries**: R1 and R3 for f7 and f8 are near-identical points (submitted independently), confirming output stability rather than introducing noise.  
- **Boundary artefacts**: f4 exhibits catastrophic output collapse (−27 to −30) at boundary region (x1→1, x2/x3→0), reflecting a domain-specific feature of the synthetic function design.
- **R4 minimisation error**: Round 4 submitted four suboptimal queries due to incorrect minimisation EI formulation. These are retained in the dataset as valid observations but treated as exploration points rather than exploitation results.

## Collection Process

**How was the data collected?**  
Two sources:

1. **Initial data**: provided as `.npy` files by the Imperial College BBO portal at project start. Loaded via `np.load()`.
2. **Submitted query data**: generated by the participant using GP-EI acquisition functions, submitted one point per function per round to the portal, and returned as input–output pairs plus cumulative `.npy` files.

**What mechanisms or procedures were used to collect the data?**  
- **Rounds 1–4**: Gaussian Process with Matérn-5/2 ARD kernel + WhiteKernel; EI acquisition (Round 4: inadvertently minimisation; corrected R5)
- **Round 5**: Corrected maximisation EI; NN finite-difference gradients supplemented GP for f5, f7
- **Rounds 6–10**: GP-EI (35 L-BFGS-B restarts, top-35 of 5,000 warm-start candidates) with per-function strategic override rules derived from empirical gradient analysis of the accumulated dataset

**Who was involved in the data collection process?**  
Queries designed by Gian Franco Cattaneo using scikit-learn, scipy, and numpy. Portal evaluation performed by Imperial College London systems.

**Over what timeframe was the data collected?**  
March–June 2026 (Rounds 1–10, Weeks 12–21 of the Executive Master programme).

## Preprocessing / Cleaning / Labelling

**Was any preprocessing of the data done?**  
- `StandardScaler` (zero mean, unit variance) applied to input arrays `X` before GP fitting in all rounds
- `normalize_y=True` applied within `GaussianProcessRegressor`
- No clipping, imputation, or transformation of output values `Y` (used raw as returned by portal)

**Is the software used to preprocess the data available?**  
Yes — see `requirements.txt`. All preprocessing occurs inline within the round notebooks.

## Uses

**What tasks has the dataset been used for?**  
- Gaussian Process surrogate model fitting (regression)
- Bayesian optimisation via Expected Improvement acquisition
- Neural network surrogate training (MLPRegressor 2×32 ReLU) for gradient/sensitivity analysis (Rounds 4–5)
- Empirical per-round gradient law derivation (Rounds 6–10)
- Cumulative best tracking and convergence monitoring across 10 rounds

**Is there anything about the composition of the dataset that might affect future uses?**  
- With N≤50 per function, GP posteriors in high dimensions (d=6, d=8) carry significant uncertainty. Any analysis claiming definitive landscape characterisation should be treated with caution.
- Exploitation concentration from Round 5 onward means large regions of the domain remain unsampled, particularly near boundaries and in unexplored orthants.
- The dataset is specific to these eight synthetic functions and does not generalise to other optimisation problems without revalidation.

**Are there tasks for which the dataset should not be used?**  
- Production deployment decisions (functions are synthetic and do not represent real industrial processes)
- Training general-purpose ML models (dataset is too small and domain-specific)

## Limitations

1. **Small N relative to dimensionality**: 9–10 submitted observations per function (plus initial data). GP confidence intervals are wide; ARD length-scale estimates may be unstable, particularly for d=6 and d=8 where N/d < 2.
2. **Exploitation concentration bias**: from Round 5 onward, the search became heavily localised. The dataset systematically underrepresents boundary regions, unexplored orthants, and mid-range coordinates.
3. **Single-point queries**: one query per function per round imposes slow convergence relative to batch BO approaches. With 10 submitted queries per function, the effective exploration budget is critically limited at d=8.
4. **Function-specific scope**: insights (f4 boundary collapse, f7 sparse structure, f8 x3 gradient) are specific to these synthetic functions and do not constitute transferable principles.
5. **Temporal gradient assumption**: the empirical −0.003/round law for f7 and +0.002/round for f8 are extrapolated beyond the observed range in Round 10. Deceleration already evident in both functions suggests the true optima may be close; the gradient law may not hold for further rounds.
6. **Maximisation assumption**: if any function has not been negated by the portal, the acquisition function direction would be incorrect. This is assumed to be correctly handled per project specification.
